The problem

One video for every search. And it plays muted.

Sponsored Brands video sits at the top of Amazon search and starts playing without sound. So the first seconds of picture are what the shopper actually gets.

Someone searches hot dog roasting sticks, and your ad opens on a marshmallow. The next shopper searches vegetable roasting sticks and gets the same marshmallow.

The hack

Change the first 4 seconds. Nothing else.

The skill replaces only the picture up to your video's first cut, about 4 seconds in, and puts the keyword on screen. Everything after the cut is your video, untouched.

The sound never moves. Each version keeps your soundtrack and your length, so it never sounds like two videos taped together.

The six openings

Six searches, six openings. Then one video.

The demo product is a pack of roasting sticks. Each keyword names a different food, feature or place, so each one gets its own scene. At the first cut, all six turn into the same video.

All six openings are AI-generated in Genrupt, with the product's own photos as the reference. For this demo, each one was picked from two or three takes.

  • HOT DOG A dad and his son at a campfire, two hot dogs on the sticks.
  • VEGETABLE A mom and her son holding vegetable skewers over the fire.
  • S'MORES A boy toasts a marshmallow, a plate of crackers and chocolate in his other hand.
  • EXTENDABLE One hand holds the handle while the other pulls the stick out to full length.
  • BACKYARD FIRE PIT A couple at a fire bowl in the backyard, string lights behind them.
  • FIREPLACE A mom and her son roasting marshmallows at a brick fireplace.
How it works

Four steps. Claude Code does the work.

You bring the video and say yes to the price. It does the rest and shows you every version.

1

Give it your video, ASIN and keywords

3 to 10 keywords. No list? Claude reads your listing and suggests keywords that change the picture: a different food, use, person or place.

> /keyword-openers my-video.mp4, ASIN B0XXXXXXXX, keywords: hot dog roasting sticks, vegetable roasting sticks, fireplace roasting sticks
2

It writes one scene per keyword

It reads your video first, so each scene matches its light, its people and its look. You see every scene as one line before anything is made.

  • hot dog roasting sticks: a dad and his son holding two hot dogs over the same campfire
  • vegetable roasting sticks: a mom and her son with vegetable skewers by the fire
3

Genrupt creates each scene

One 5-second AI clip per keyword, made with your product's photos as the reference. Claude shows you the price first and waits for your yes.

It swaps the opening and checks every file

The scene goes in up to your video's first cut, the keyword goes on screen, and every version is checked. Then you get one page to review them side by side.

Running it on Amazon

One ad group per keyword.

In Sponsored Brands video, give every keyword its own version, and keep your original running next to it.

  1. Make one ad group per keyword.
  2. Target it on exact match, so the shopper who sees the hot dog opening is the shopper who typed hot dog.
  3. Upload that keyword's version as the ad group's video.
  4. Keep your original video running on the same keyword. It is your control.
  5. After 1 to 2 weeks, compare click-through rate per keyword against the original. Keep the winners, cut the rest.
