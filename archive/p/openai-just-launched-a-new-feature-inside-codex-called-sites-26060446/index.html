OpenAI just launched a new feature inside Codex called Sites, and it is one of the...

Basically, it allows you to take an idea, a spec document, data analysis, or a prompt, and turn them into a live, interactive website or app, complete with a URL you can share within your organizational workspace.

Over the past few months, I have built quite a few AI processes and agents in Codex for organizations I work with. One of the reasons I often choose to work there, in some cases even over Claude Code, is how incredibly easy it is to share internal tools, agents, and workflows among team members.

Because when you are working with a team, it is not enough for the AI to build something good. Others need to be able to open it, test it, comment on it, and actually use it in practice.

Until today, even after the system wrote great code for you, you still had to figure out how to host it so others could see it. GitHub, Vercel, Replit, permissions, sharing, maintenance. For many teams, especially those without a technical background, this is exactly where good ideas get stuck.

With Codex Sites, it becomes much simpler. In practice, you just go into Codex, select Sites from the left menu (or just start a prompt with @sites), and type out what you want to build:

• "Build me a live browser dashboard from this data analysis."
• "Turn this spec document into an internal team site."
• "Create a page where the team can review creative ideas and leave feedback."
• "Build an interactive workspace for this launch plan."

Codex starts building the site, uses the files and context it has, and ultimately generates a working product that you can open and share with a single click inside the Workspace.

I played around with it today, and my initial takeaway is that Codex is starting to become a complete end to end workspace. It is no longer just a chat that writes code. It is a platform that lets you build tools, host them, and share them internally without ever leaving to use another tool.

True, right now sharing is limited to your internal workspace and is not a public site for the whole world. But for organizations and internal teams, this is a massive feature.

I really hope Claude launches something similar soon.
