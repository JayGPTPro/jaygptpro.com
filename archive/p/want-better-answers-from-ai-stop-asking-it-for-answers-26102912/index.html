Want better answers from AI? Stop asking it for answers

Ever asked ChatGPT something that really matters to you, only to get a response that's well-written, technically correct, and could have been written for literally anyone?
And you think: "This doesn't really help me."

But the problem isn't the AI. The problem is that we're asking it to give us an answer before it actually understands what we need.
When AI doesn't have enough context, it fills in the gaps with generic assumptions. And that's exactly why so many AI answers sound the same.

So why not just explain everything upfront?
Because it's hard. Sitting in front of a blank screen and trying to explain everything, what you need, why, what you've already tried, what matters to you, is exhausting. And you always forget something.
But answering questions? That's easy. It's how we're wired.
A good conversation with someone who asks the right questions brings out things we never would have thought to write down ourselves.

So next time you need something from AI, don't start by asking for answers. Start by asking it to ask YOU questions.
Tell it your goal and let it interview you.

Try telling your AI something like this:
"I want to achieve [your goal]. Before giving me an answer, ask me questions, one at a time, until you understand my situation, what I've already tried, and what matters most to me. Only when you have enough context, suggest a way forward."

This works with ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude, even on their free plans.

And here's something that makes it even easier:
Don't type your answers. Just speak them.
Use the microphone in the app and talk like you're sending a voice message. It's so much easier to explain things out loud than to put them into writing. And sometimes, that's when the most important details come out.

Try it today with something you've been struggling with.
Even if you don't get the perfect solution right away, you'll notice something interesting: the questions themselves help you understand what you actually want.
And by the end of the conversation, all those scattered thoughts in your head are suddenly organized right in front of you.
