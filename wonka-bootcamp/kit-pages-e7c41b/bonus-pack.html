Eleven skills that take Wonka a step past the bootcamp

The bootcamp taught Wonka to build one product's package, room by room. This pack teaches him what comes after: the next product in one run, the gallery dressed for a season or a market, twenty more scenes for your ads, a landing page, a film. Some of it is video and other creative work the bootcamp never covered.

One rule holds all eleven together. Every skill in the pack makes files from the product already on your line. It reads the reference sheet you locked, the brief you approved, the research, the frames you chose, and it produces images, videos or pages you can use. None of it writes listing copy and none of it gives advice. That is what the 74 free skills are for.

Before you start

  • A product that finished the line. Most skills need the locked reference sheet and the shipped gallery. A product still mid-bootcamp gets one line from Wonka and then he continues, nothing is blocked.
  • The two machines from Day 1. The Genrupt MCP and your OpenAI key, exactly as the kit uses them. /machines-check still proves both are alive.
  • For the two video skills only: ffmpeg, Node 18 or newer, numpy and whisper-cpp. The pack ships a check that names what is missing:
bash .claude/skills/asin-to-video-autopilot/setup/check-env.sh

Install, once

  1. Download wonka-bonus-pack.zip and unzip it anywhere. It unpacks to a folder called wonka-bonus-pack.
  2. Open a terminal in that folder and run the installer with the path to your own Wonka folder, the one that holds your factory/:
bash install.sh /path/to/your/Wonka
  1. Open your Wonka folder in Claude Code and say:
What new skills do I have?
Tip. The installer refuses to touch anything that is not a pack skill, so your kit stays exactly as it was. Running it twice is safe. Wonka's own updates leave the pack alone, and the pack leaves them alone.

What is in the box

Each card says what comes out, what you say to Wonka, what it usually costs, and where the files land. Costs are estimates from the skills' own figures; every skill says the real number before it spends, and stops at its cap rather than asking you mid-run.

The line, after graduation

/listing-autopilot . The whole line in one run

The next product, from the ASIN and your photos to a tested package, with three stops where you decide: the lock and the recipe, the picks, the verdict. Everything else is Wonka's call, made by the rooms' own rules and logged.

Say "autopilot B0XXXXXXXX", "run the whole line", "next product, fast"

Costs the line's own costs, estimated at about $25 to $35 across both machines. Two caps, one per machine, set once in listing-autopilot.json.

Lands in the product folder, every room where it always saves, plus autopilot/report.md

/running-order . Which image goes where

Every listing slot gets one job, matched to an approved frame, ranked by what shoppers doubt (the tasting cards, the reviews). Then the shelf is drawn the way a phone shows it and read back at thumbnail size.

Say "what order do I upload these in", "which image should be image 2", "how does the set look on a phone"

Costs nothing. It reads and it draws.

Lands in upload/running-order.md and upload/shelf-strip.png

/refresh . The quarterly return to the shelf

Snapshots your listing and every competitor the research named, diffs them against the last visit (frames added, replaced, reordered; prices and ratings moved), reads the new reviews, and ends with one asset to make or replace, with its evidence.

Say "what changed on the shelf", "check my competitors again", "are my images still good"

Costs usually nothing. A fresh market analysis is optional and quoted first.

Lands in refresh/[date]/refresh.md

Dressing the gallery you already have

/santa . The gallery for Christmas

Your shipped gallery dressed for Christmas with the product untouched and the main image left alone. Warm lights, a little greenery, at most three props, never darker. A before and after sheet, and the originals kept for January.

Say "christmas versions of my gallery", "Q4 images", "holiday images"

Costs about $0.15 for a six-frame preview, roughly $1 for the upload set. Cap $2 per run.

Lands in seasonal/christmas/

/seasons . The same engine for every other occasion

Valentine's, Mother's Day, Father's Day, Easter, Halloween, Thanksgiving, the Fourth of July, back to school, summer, New Year, or an occasion you name. Same two rules as Christmas: around the product, never on it; bright, never moody.

Say "mother's day images", "halloween gallery", "dress the gallery for [event]"

Costs the same as Santa. Cap $2 per run.

Lands in seasonal/[season]/, one folder per season

/localize-gallery . Your frames in another language

The shipped gallery rebuilt for another marketplace: the same pictures, every headline and label redrawn in the target language, units converted where a frame shows them, the product untouched. Every rendered line is proofread at native resolution, character by character, before it counts.

Say "the gallery in German", "images for amazon.de", "Japanese version of the listing images"

Costs under $0.25 for the proof of a seven-frame gallery, about $1.20 for the upload set. Cap $2 to $3 per market.

Lands in localized/[market]/ with copy.md, which you can hand to a native reader before the upload set

New frames from the locked product

/lifestyle-pack . Twenty scenes, no words on them

The same locked product in twenty real settings drawn from your buyer's day, in the three ratios (square, wide, tall), with no text, no badges, no logos, so the frames can carry any words later. For ads, email, social, the brand store, the landing page.

Say "I need images for ads", "lifestyle images", "something for the brand store"

Costs about $0.25 to $0.60 for all twenty. Cap $3 per run.

Lands in lifestyle/, sorted by context and ratio

/steal-this-frame . Their idea, your product

Point at a competitor's strongest frame. Wonka writes down what it does (the composition, the angle, the job of the text, why it works) and builds the same idea from scratch on your locked product. Their photograph never reaches the model. The result is a candidate for your gallery, sent through /inspect like any other.

Say "make me one like theirs", "their image 2 is great", "rebuild this with my product"

Costs under $0.10 for two or three candidates. Cap $0.50.

Lands in steal/[competitor]-[slot]/ with the teardown beside the frames

Beyond the listing

/landing-page . A page built from the package

A premium product page designed from the creative the factory made: the facts from the research, the real review quotes, the images from the shipped gallery and the lifestyle pack, the palette pulled from your own frames. One self-contained HTML file with a gallery, reviews, FAQ, email capture and a sticky mobile buy bar.

Say "build a landing page", "a page for my ads", "landing page for [product]"

Costs nothing to build. Nothing on the page is invented; a section with no real data is left out.

Lands in landing-page/index.html

/asin-to-video-autopilot . A finished 30-second film

The product on your line turned into a listing film: a hook, real use with people, one signature camera move, diegetic sound, narration and music, quality-checked at every stage. Zero questions during the run; one cost cap set once.

Say "make a video for this ASIN", "product video autopilot", "listing video for B0..."

Costs credits on the Genrupt machine, mostly the master render; the skill quotes every paid step before it runs it and stops at the cap. One path comes back: out/FINAL-[product].mp4.

Lands in video/runs/[ASIN]-[date]/out/

/asin-to-video-copilot . The same film, with you in the decisions

Same pipeline, same quality gates, different seat. Eight decisions are yours, grouped so you sit with Wonka twice and the machine works alone in between: the traps, the direction, the reference clip, the probe frame, the master prompt, then the door and the music. Run it when a product deserves the best film you can make.

Say "co-pilot", "with me", "I want to choose"

Costs the same as the autopilot; the cap is the whole money conversation.

Lands in the same run folder, with your decisions recorded in the report

What they all share

  • They run on the factory, never on a cold ASIN. A skill never asks for something status.md already holds.
  • Each one writes in its own folder and never over another skill's file. Your images/, edits/ and final/ are never touched.
  • The price is said before it is spent. An estimate first, a cap always, and the run stops at the cap instead of asking you mid-run.
  • Every frame is looked at against the reference before it counts. A wrong product does not leave the factory, however pretty the light.
  • Wonka's voice, exactly as in the kit.

A good first evening

Three skills that cost nothing or nearly nothing and show you the pack's shape in under an hour:

  1. /running-order on the product you finished. Free. You will see your own gallery at phone size for the first time.
  2. /refresh on the same product. Free on the first visit, and it becomes the baseline for every visit after.
  3. /santa at the preview setting. About fifteen cents, and the before and after sheet tells you in one glance how the seasonal engine thinks.
Don't start with the autopilot on a product that never went through the line. It refuses a product with no real photos and says so, but the better first run is a product you already know.
